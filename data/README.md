# Dataset Notes

## This data is synthetic, and here's why

The assignment ships with a sample dataset of ~100 real transcripts, but it was not
provided to me for this exercise. Per the assignment's own FAQ:

> "Can I generate additional data? Yes. If you feel you need more transcripts to
> validate your approach, demonstrate a pattern, or test at scale, you're welcome
> to generate synthetic data using LLMs or any other method. Just be transparent
> about what's original and what you generated, and explain why you needed more."

So: **100% of `transcripts.jsonl` is synthetic**, generated by
[`scripts/generate_transcripts.py`](../scripts/generate_transcripts.py). None of it
is derived from or references real customer data.

## What's in it

103 fictional B2B SaaS call transcripts for a fictional product ("Meridian"),
split across the three call types the assignment describes:

| Type | Count | Who's talking |
|---|---|---|
| `support` | 34 | Support agent <-> customer |
| `external` | 33 | Account manager <-> customer |
| `internal` | 36 | Cross-functional (eng, PM, support, AM) |

## Why it's not just random noise

A categorization/sentiment pipeline is only as convincing as the patterns it has to
find. Flat, independent, randomly-generated transcripts would make for a boring
demo — every category would be equally distributed, sentiment would have no trend,
and there'd be nothing for the "bonus insights" section to surface. So three
storylines are deliberately woven through the data, with no explicit labels (the
pipeline has to find them from the text):

1. **A recurring export bug.** A CSV/report export silently truncates on datasets
   over 50k rows. It surfaces in support calls starting week 1, escalates into an
   internal engineering triage in week 2-3, gets pulled forward after a customer
   ties it to renewal risk, ships a fix around week 6, and support sentiment
   visibly recovers from week 7 on. This is meant to produce a real
   topic-cluster + sentiment-dip + cross-channel-correlation story.
2. **A competitor threat.** A handful of renewal conversations mid-dataset
   mention "Lumina Analytics" undercutting on price, which triggers an internal
   competitive-strategy sync.
3. **A feature launch.** "Smart Alerts" ships in the back half of the dataset,
   driving upsell enthusiasm in external calls and an internal launch-planning call.

Everything else (roughly 60% of calls) is independent, single-session content
covering a standard topic taxonomy per call type (billing, SSO/login, performance,
integrations, onboarding, security/compliance, cancellation risk, QBRs, contract
terms, incident postmortems, sprint planning, hiring, etc.) so clustering/topic
detection has real categories to find beyond just the three storylines above.

## Schema

Each line in `transcripts.jsonl` is one call:

```json
{
  "call_id": "CALL-0001",
  "call_type": "support | external | internal",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "duration_minutes": 12,
  "account_name": "fictional company or internal team name",
  "participants": ["Speaker Name", "..."],
  "transcript": "Speaker: line\nSpeaker: line\n...",
  "word_count": 143
}
```

`transcripts_index.csv` is a flat index of the same records (minus transcript text)
for quick scanning/filtering.

## Regenerating

```bash
cd scripts
python3 generate_transcripts.py
```

The script is seeded (`random.seed(42)`) for reproducibility. Dates are spread over
a 90-day window starting 2026-07-01, with the three storylines pinned to specific
day ranges (see comments in the script) and everything else randomly distributed.
